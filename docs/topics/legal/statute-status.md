# Statute status and repeal

> Summary: Registry of in-force and repealed Acts, the 2024 criminal-law transition, authoritative texts and amendment notes.

## Context
The research manifest lists the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973 among the core Acts without noting that it has been replaced. A legal assistant that serves repealed text as current law is worse than one that returns nothing. Status is data: curated in one file, applied at ingestion, and re-checked when the answer is built.

## Details

### Aliases (`data/registry/aliases.yaml`)
Aliases point at registry IDs rather than repeating titles, so a rename happens in one place:
```yaml
- alias: CrPC
  expands_to: [crpc-1973, bnss-2023]
- alias: TP Act
  expands_to: [tpa-1882]
- alias: NI Act
  expands_to: [nia-1881]
```

### Temporal rule for the criminal codes
When a question concerns an offence, an FIR, an investigation or a trial, extract the event date. Before 2024-07-01 the old code may govern, and pending proceedings may continue under it; on or after that date the new code applies. When the date is unknown, say that both regimes exist and that which one applies depends on the date and the stage of proceedings. Never pick one silently.

### Section-level mapping (IPC to BNS and similar)
Only from an official correspondence table, ingested as a source with its URL, and never generated by a model. Until such a table is ingested, the system names the successor Act but not the successor section.

### Authoritative text and translations
Authoritative texts of central Acts are in English under Article 348(1)(b) of the Constitution, and authoritative Hindi translations are published under the Authoritative Texts (Central Laws) Act, 1973. Any other translation, including ours, is a convenience translation and must be labelled as one. When answering in another language, quote the source in its original language.

### Amendments and "as of" dates
Statute chunks carry the India Code text as retrieved, with `retrieved_at`, plus footnotes parsed into `amendment_notes`. Answers say "based on the text retrieved on <date>" and never claim a section is current beyond that. State amendments — to the Registration Act or a Stamp Act in a given state, for instance — are out of scope unless that state's text is ingested, and the answer must say so when the question is state-specific.
